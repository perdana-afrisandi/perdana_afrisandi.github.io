Omnichannel Promo Cannibalization & Margin Erosion Analytics

Evaluating baseline sales lift, sister-SKU cannibalization, and post-promo dip to eliminate margin-destructive retail promotions.

Section 01

1. Overview & Business Context

Retail and FMCG companies spend up to 20% of gross revenue on trade promotions (flash sales, price cuts, vouchers). While unit sales almost always spike during promo weeks, net profit frequently drops. Naive dashboards celebrate 'Gross Promo Revenue' without realizing that loyal customers simply stockpiled early or switched from regular-priced sister SKUs.

This project builds a rigorous Commercial Promo Evaluation Framework that decomposes gross promo volume into True Incremental Lift vs. Cannibalization and Stockpiling.

Section 02

2. Data Sources & Star Schema Modeling

Combines the public Dunnhumby Retail Dataset with synthetic COGS, promo mechanics, and store cluster tables across 120 supermarkets:

  • fact_pos_weekly_sales: Store-SKU weekly unit volume, shelf price, paid price, discount depth, and gross margin.
  • dim_sku_hierarchy: Brand, sub-category, pack-size tier (e.g., 100g vs 200g sister SKUs), and unit COGS.
  • dim_promo_campaigns: Promo mechanic type (Direct Price Cut, Multi-Buy Bundle, Minimum Basket Voucher) and display location.
Section 03

3. Data Quality & Technical Challenges Solved

  • Baseline Estimation Bias: Including out-of-stock weeks or previous promo weeks distorts the rolling baseline. Solved by filtering out OOS and promo flags before computing an 8-week seasonal rolling median.
  • Sister-SKU Halo vs. Cannibalization: Measuring whether a discount on Brand-A 200g caused a simultaneous volume drop in full-margin Brand-A 100g within the same store cluster.
  • Post-Promo Dip (Pantry Loading): Accounting for the 2-week sales trough immediately following a deep-discount event.
Section 04

4. Analytical Walkthrough & SQL Implementation

  • Step 1 — Non-Promo Baseline Modeling: Computed expected weekly baseline units per SKU-Store using SQL window functions adjusted for payday seasonality.
  • Step 2 — Price Elasticity of Demand (PED): Estimated SKU-level price elasticity using log-log regression in Python (`statsmodels`).
  • Step 3 — Net Incremental P&L Bridge: Calculated True Net Promo ROI = (Incremental Margin) - (Subsidized Margin on Baseline Units) - (Cannibalized Sister SKU Margin) - (Post-Promo Dip Loss).
-- Production SQL Validation & Analytical Query
SELECT sku_id, brand_name, promo_mechanic,
       SUM(actual_units - baseline_units) AS gross_lift_units,
       SUM(actual_net_margin_idr - baseline_expected_margin_idr - sister_sku_cannibalized_margin_idr - post_promo_dip_margin_loss_idr) AS true_net_incremental_profit_idr
FROM `synth_retail.mart_promo_profitability_bridge`
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
ORDER BY true_net_incremental_profit_idr ASC;
Section 05

5. Key Takeaways & Quantified Business Impact

  • 27% of deep-discount campaigns (>25% price cut) generated negative net profit despite +40% unit volume growth.
  • Flagship 200g coffee packs suffered a 65% cannibalization rate against higher-margin 100g packs during standalone price cuts.
  • Replacing standalone price cuts on inelastic SKUs with cross-category minimum-basket bundling increased Average Order Value (AOV) by +18% and improved category net margin by +4.8%.
